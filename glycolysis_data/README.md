# Glycolytic oscillator data (Wolf2000, Bier2000)

This directory holds simulated trajectories for two glycolytic oscillator models,
generated by `bio_chem_attempts.py`:

| File | Model | BioModels ID | State variables |
|------|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| `wolf2000_trajectories.csv` | Wolf, Passarge, Somsen, Snoep, Westerhoff & Heinrich (2000) yeast glycolysis | BIOMD0000000206 | `s1`(Glucose), `at`(ATP), `s2`(F16P), `s3`(Triose), `na`(NAD), `s4`(3PG), `s5`(Pyruvate), `s6`(Acetaldehyde), `s6o`(ext. Acetaldehyde) |
| `bier2000_trajectories.csv` | Bier, Bakker & Westerhoff (2000) two coupled glycolytic cells | BIOMD0000000254 | `G1`,`T1`,`G2`,`T2` (glucose/ATP in two diffusively coupled cells) |

## Why SR-GB+CSNP finds no polynomial invariant here (and why that is correct)

Running the invariant-recovery pipeline on either model returns **no polynomial
invariant**. This is the expected, physically correct outcome, not a pipeline
failure, for two reasons:

1. **The moiety conservation laws are already eliminated in the model coordinates.**
   The Wolf2000 model conserves the adenine pool (ATP + ADP = `atot` = 4 mM) and
   the NAD pool (NAD + NADH = `ntot` = 1 mM). But the SBML does **not** carry ADP
   or NADH as state variables: it substitutes `ADP = atot - at` and
   `NADH = ntot - na` directly into the rate laws. So the only linear conservation
   laws the system has were used up to reduce its dimension, and **no non-trivial
   linear or polynomial relation survives among the recorded columns**
   (`s1, at, s2, ..., s6o`). SVD of the design matrix is full rank, correctly.

2. **These are dissipative, open, limit-cycle systems.** Glucose enters at a fixed
   input flux and product leaves; energy and mass are not conserved along a
   trajectory. The scientifically interesting object is the *oscillation* (the limit
   cycle, its nullclines, its period), which is a property of the **dynamics**, not
   a conserved quantity of the state.

### Why we do NOT reconstruct ADP/NADH to force a "hit"

One could append `ADP = 4 - at` and `NADH = 1 - na` as extra columns; then
`at + ADP - 4 = 0` and `na + NADH - 1 = 0` would be "recovered." We deliberately do
**not** do this. Those columns are exact linear functions of columns already
present, so the recovered law is a tautology injected by us, not a relation
discovered from the data. It measures nothing about the method and would be
misleading in the paper.

## How the state of the art actually uses this data

A literature check (July 2026) shows the glycolytic oscillator is a **standard
benchmark for data-driven discovery of governing equations / dynamics**, i.e. for
SINDy-family and symmetry-informed equation-discovery methods that learn the ODE
right-hand sides, and for testing robustness to noise on oscillatory data. It is
**not** used as a conservation-law / algebraic-invariant benchmark, precisely
because (as above) the interesting content is dynamical, not algebraic.

- Brunton, Proctor & Kutz, *Discovering governing equations from data by sparse
  identification of nonlinear dynamical systems*, PNAS 2016
  (glycolytic oscillator as a SINDy test case).
- Yang, Rao et al., *Symmetry-Informed Governing Equation Discovery*, NeurIPS 2024
  (Sel'kov glycolytic oscillator benchmark under noise), arXiv:2405.16756.

Dedicated **conservation-law** discovery methods instead benchmark on systems that
genuinely have invariants, and they treat "no invariant exists" as a case a good
method must handle by **abstaining** (no false positive), e.g.:

- *From Data to Laws: Neural Discovery of Conservation Laws Without False Positives*
  (NGCG pipeline), arXiv:2603.20474: 9 systems, 4 with conservation laws and 5
  without; a constancy gate + diversity filter give FDR = 0 and correctly output
  *no law* on the invariant-free systems.
- *Towards robust data-driven automated recovery of symbolic conservation laws from
  limited data*, Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol. 2024 / arXiv:2403.04889 (singular-vector
  stability for conservation-law recovery from limited data).

## Takeaway for this repository

Glycolysis is best treated here as an **abstention / negative control**: a system a
conservation-law method should report *no invariant* for, demonstrating the absence
of false positives, rather than as a positive recovery benchmark. The positive
conservation-law benchmark in this repo is the CRN / MAPK cascade family in
`crn_data/`, where every phospho-form is an independent measured species and the
moiety conservation laws (total kinase, total phosphatase) are genuine multi-species
relations to be discovered, not reconstructed. See `crn_data/` and
`bio_chem_attempts.py`.
